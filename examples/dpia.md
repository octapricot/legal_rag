## Analysis

A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is required under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) when the processing of personal data is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. This includes monitoring publicly accessible areas on a large scale, especially when using optic-electronic devices [GDPR Recital 91], systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas on a large scale [GDPR Art. 35(3)(c)], and processing special categories of data on a large scale [GDPR Art. 35(3)(b)]. The controller should carry out the DPIA prior to the processing and seek advice from the Data Protection Officer where designated [GDPR Recital 90, GDPR Art. 35(2)]. If necessary, the controller shall review the processing in accordance with the DPIA when there is a change of the risk represented by processing operations [GDPR Art. 35(11)]. The EDPB provides additional interpretive guidance for DPIAs in specific contexts such as video devices [EDPB Guidelines 03/2019 on Video Devices, §10], facial recognition technology [EDPB Guidelines 04/2023 on Facial Recognition, §3.2.5.1], connected vehicles [EDPB Guidelines 01/2020 on Connected Vehicles, §2.4.3], and health data [EDPB Guidelines 03/2020 on Health Data, §5.4]

## Key Verbatim Provisions

**GDPR Recital 91**
> A data protection impact assessment is equally required for monitoring publicly accessible areas on a large scale, especially when using optic-electronic devices or for any other operations where the competent supervisory authority considers that the processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects.

**GDPR Recital 90**
> In such cases, a data protection impact assessment should be carried out by the controller prior to the processing in order to assess the particular likelihood and severity of the high risk.

**GDPR Art. 35(3)(c)**
> controllers are required to carry out data protection impact assessments if the processing constitutes a systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale.

**GDPR Art. 35(3)(b)**
> a data protection impact assessment is also required when the controller intends to process special categories of data on a large scale.

**GDPR Art. 35(2)**
> The controller shall seek the advice of the data protection officer, where designated, when carrying out a data protection impact assessment.

**GDPR Art. 35(11)**
> Where necessary, the controller shall carry out a review to assess if processing is performed in accordance with the data protection impact assessment at least when there is a change of the risk represented by processing operations.

**EDPB Guidelines 03/2019 on Video Devices, §10**
> According to Article 35 (1) GDPR controllers are required to conduct data protection impact assessments (DPIA) when a type of data processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

**EDPB Guidelines 04/2023 on Facial Recognition, §3.2.5.1**
> A data protection impact assessment (DPIA) before the use of FRT is a mandatory requirement since the type of processing, in particular, using new technologies, and taking into account the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

**EDPB Guidelines 01/2020 on Connected Vehicles, §2.4.3**
> Given the scale and sensitivity of the personal data that can be generated via connected vehicles; it is likely that processing – particularly in situations where personal data are processed outside of the vehicle-will often result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals. Where this is the case, industry participants will be required to perform a data protection impact assessment (DPIA)to identify and mitigate the risks as detailed in art.35 and 36GDPR.

**EDPB Guidelines 03/2020 on Health Data, §5.4 [part 2]**
> a data protection impact assessment pursuant to Article 35 GDPR must be carried out when such processing is 'likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons' pursuant to Article 35 (1) GDPR.
